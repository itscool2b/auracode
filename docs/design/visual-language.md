# Aura visual system

Status: **Halo, implemented 1 November 2026, awaiting the owner's visual lock**. The owner asked for this to be the last full styling pass. It covers the light-only website and the desktop app in both appearances. Local browser verification and native Windows checks are recorded in the review record. Do not treat the current styling or its captures as an approved golden baseline until the owner locks the design. Halo supersedes the earlier Aura proposal of 30 October and the old Graphite, Orbit and Shallows directions. Historical Design Lab screens are studies, not product authority.

The current review record is [checkpoint-2026-11-01-halo.md](checkpoint-2026-11-01-halo.md). Earlier records, [checkpoint-2026-11-01.md](checkpoint-2026-11-01.md) and [checkpoint-2026-10-30.md](checkpoint-2026-10-30.md), are historical.

## Direction

Halo began as glass windows lit by each agent's brand glow. The owner retired every glow on 2 November 2026: "aura" in the name does not mean glowing colour, and none may be added again, on the website or in the app. Depth now comes from tone, hairlines, texture, type contrast and real product imagery. The website's night bands and the hero stage are rounded slabs set into the paper, the hero window stands on a cool-grey stage ruled in a fine grid, and the night bands share one hairline grid. The brand colours in `packages/ui/src/brand.ts` mark agents only (their logos and chips), never light. The desktop has no glow at all (owner, 2 November 2026: "aura" in the name does not mean glowing colour; never add one). Its live state is the working line in the transcript; nothing moves on the composer (owner, 6 November 2026, retiring the sea lion and turtle that swam along its rim). In Aura, auracode's own theme, accent, focus and selection stay neutral, per the owner's 1 November decision; each agent's theme carries its agent's colour there instead (owner, 3 November 2026), and Claude Code's is the default (owner, 4 November 2026; see Themes below).

The website takes its cleanliness and organization from the audited SpaceXAI, Harvey, Vercel and Aside landing pages. It matches Typesafe's uniqueness without copying it, and it uses rich, purposeful animation. The desktop keeps the audited Devin organization and is glass throughout, over the native window material. Adapt patterns to auracode's own product and capabilities. Do not copy reference brands, artwork or content, and do not imply unsupported functionality. Preserve the actual auracode logo, including its sea lion and turtle artwork, and its app icons and favicons. Do not add independent decorative animals, swimming or water motifs; the working companion that swam along the composer's rim was retired on 6 November 2026, so there is no exception.

## No eyebrows

Never add eyebrows. That means no small label above a heading or section. It covers uppercase or mono micro-labels, numbered kickers such as "THE WORK / 01", "brand / section" lines, category labels and badges. Headings stand on their own. `.github/scripts/check-styles.mjs` enforces this for the website, the desktop and the shared primitives. It rejects uppercase text transforms, eyebrow, kicker and overline class names, and generated capitalized `content` labels.

## Shared foundation

`packages/ui/src/graphite/tokens.css` is the shared token source. `graphite/tokens.ts` and its tests mirror palettes where code needs them. Existing `--g-*` names are compatibility names, not permission to restore the former Graphite direction. Keep color, typography, spacing, radii, glass, motion, states, code and terminal values semantic and centrally defined. Do not introduce a parallel token system.

Glass tokens:

- **Window.** `--g-window` is `#0b0b0c` dark and `#efeff1` light, matching the native window's base color. `--g-window-tint` washes the material behind the sidebar.
- **Fills.** Translucent fills sit on top in a fixed order: `--g-glass-layer` for panes, `--g-glass-bar` for sticky toolbars, `--g-glass-pop` for menus, popovers, dialogs and the composer, and `--g-glass-card` for grouped lists and cards. `--g-glass-raised` is for raised thumbs only: a checked segment, an open popover trigger, a selected tab. `--g-glass-control`, `-hover` and `-active` are for controls, and `--g-glass-selected` is for the open row.
- **Edges.** Edges are inset hairlines, `--g-glass-rim` and `--g-glass-rim-strong`, with a `--g-glass-highlight` top edge on panes.
- **Blur and shadow.** Blur comes only from `--g-blur-bar`, `--g-blur-pop` and `--g-blur-scrim`. Shadows come only from `--g-shadow-control`, `-primary`, `-layer` and `-pop`. `tokens.test.ts` rejects other blur and shadow values.
- **Contrast.** `tokens.test.ts` composites every fill over the worst plausible material: a light window over a black desktop and a dark window over a white one. It requires 4.5:1 for primary, secondary and tertiary text on each result.

Radii form a concentric family: 6, 10, 14, 18 and 22px, plus pill. Every control is a pill: buttons, chips, segmented controls, tabs, switches and picker triggers. Inputs and menu rows use 10px; panes, menus and grouped lists 14px; transcript and setup cards 18px; the composer 22px. Nested radii stay concentric.

Geist Variable is the interface and prose face on the desktop and the website. Geist Mono sets machine-generated text. Fraunces Italic sets accent phrases on the website only (owner, 3 November 2026: "something sexier" than the Instrument Serif Italic it replaces). It is a high-contrast display italic, bundled from `@fontsource-variable/fraunces` as its italic optical-size cut only, so the website loads one accent file. Its optical size follows the type size, and display settings pin it at 144, the sharpest; it sets at weight 360 with -0.028em tracking. The wheel's facts use it too. Desktop chrome is 13/20px, prose 15/24px, metadata 12/16px, headings 16/22px and titles 20/28px. The chat's own text (answers, sent messages, question options and the composer) is `--g-text-chat`/`--g-leading-chat`, 14/22px, and answers run the whole column instead of stopping at 600px (owner, 4 November 2026: fit more text in, as Codex does). Settings > General > Text size offers Compact, the default, and Comfortable, which sets the chat at the prose size as before (`data-text-size="comfortable"` on the root). Keep success, warning and danger distinct and verify text contrast on its real surface.

Dark background and first four surfaces are `#101112`, `#141516`, `#1c1d1f`, `#202226` and `#292b2f`. The light counterparts are `#fafafa`, `#ffffff`, `#f4f4f5`, `#f0f0f2` and `#e8e8eb`. Code, terminal, diff, selection and status tokens keep explicit values for both modes. Do not assume a dark code or terminal surface in light mode.

Desktop appearance defaults to System, with profile-local persisted Light and Dark overrides in the versioned setup preferences. Follow OS changes while System is selected. Keep the document root, native window and menus, portals and terminal palette consistent with the resolved appearance. The website is permanently light, with no appearance control, theme or persisted choice, including its script-free standalone notices page. Website forms and third-party verification use light appearance. The hosted verification page stays light inside a dark desktop dialog. Preserve non-JavaScript behavior and the website's hashed content security policy.

## Themes

The desktop has a theme for every supported agent (owner, 3 November 2026: "multiple sexy ass themes ... a theme for every supported harness"). Settings > Appearance > Theme offers Aura, auracode's own neutral palette, then Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build, Pi, Antigravity, Cursor, OpenCode, oh-my-pi and Hermes. Claude Code's is the default (owner, 4 November 2026: "make the claude theme the default theme"): a profile that never chose a theme gets it, and its first frame already wears it; a saved choice, Aura included, is kept. The website's product images keep Aura's neutral palette, which suits its paper and ink. A theme is a palette layer independent of the appearance: `data-palette` beside `data-theme` on the document root, with its own dark and light sets, so System, Light and Dark work in every theme. It changes colour only, never type, spacing, radii or motion: surfaces, lines, text, filled controls, accent, focus, selection, code, terminal and diff colours, and the glass tints over the native material. It adds no glow, and menus and popovers stay solid. Each is cut from its agent's own look without copying any interface or artwork: Claude Code is warm graphite and ivory paper with terracotta for what you act on; Codex is cool slate with an ice-blue mark; Grok Build is true black and white; Pi is charcoal and amber; Antigravity is indigo night with a violet mark; Cursor is ink with a periwinkle mark; OpenCode is a terminal at night, deep green-black and phosphor green; oh-my-pi is plum with a magenta mark; Hermes is warm black and gold. The gallery shows each theme as a small window painted in its own colours for the appearance in effect, with the agent's mark beside its name; a choice applies at once, arrow keys run through the themes without losing focus, and a failed save returns to the saved theme beside the error. The choice is saved in the profile beside the appearance; a missing or unknown theme reads as the default, Claude Code's.

`packages/ui/src/graphite/themes.ts` is the source of truth. `themes.css` is generated from it (`node packages/ui/scripts/write-themes.mts`), and `themes.test.ts` fails on drift or on any contrast the base palette keeps: text on the page, every surface and the glass over the worst plausible window material, outlines and focus rings, labels on filled controls, and code on its surface. A new agent gets a theme in the same change that adds it, or Aura stands in until it does.

## Desktop composition

The window shows its native material:

- **Windows.** Acrylic on Windows 11 build 22523 and later, Mica on builds 22000 to 22522, and opaque on Windows 10. A glass window hands its caption colors back to the system, so the caption and the page read as one surface.
- **macOS.** Vibrancy, or Liquid Glass where the system offers it.
- **Linux and browser fixtures.** No material. The same layers are painted over the opaque `--g-window`.

`prefers-reduced-transparency` replaces every glass fill with its solid equivalent.

The layers:

- The sidebar sits on the window tint.
- The chat (with its docked terminal), the settings pages and the Changes panel float as translucent layers, inset 8px with a 14px radius.
- The header and sticky toolbars are blurred bars over scrolling content. The composer is a glass capsule: the popover glass under the stronger blur, a lit top edge with a short sheen under it (above the first line of text), a hairline rim and the long soft shadow. Focus brightens the rim to the strong hairline and draws no coloured ring (owner, 9 November 2026: "remove the outline of color and make this whole thing like a nice glass styling"); the caret says where typing goes. New chat's card wears the same glass.
- Menus, popovers and tooltips are solid (`--ui-menu-fill`, the `--g-overlay` surface; owner, 2 November 2026), so what is being chosen reads clearly over any content. Dialogs stay frosted.
- Grouped settings rows and transcript cards sit on cards with a hairline rim.
- The open chat and the open settings page are lit capsules. No colored side bars mark selection. Where a lit fill would take colored or secondary text under 4.5:1 on frosted glass, the selection is a rim without a fill, as on the selected changed file. The Git review panels are rimmed outlines for the same reason.

Design for a 1440 x 900 native window, minimum 960 x 640, with 52px clearance for native title controls. The sidebar is 256px expanded and 56px as a rail. At 1200px and wider, the Changes panel can resize from 320 to 560px; below that it overlays at up to 420px. The terminal remains persistent and resizable. The panel's Session tab ends with what the running session loaded, as its agent reports it (missing features B, 5 November 2026): MCP servers, each with a state dot beside its word (green connected, amber starting or waiting for a sign-in, red failed), then skills, plugins and subagents, each folded to its count; MCP servers open when one failed or waits for a sign-in. Changes reviews as well as reverts (missing features C, 5 November 2026): each diff line has a comment button in a narrow first column, shown on the row's hover or focus (always where there is no hover), with one Tab stop and the arrow keys moving between lines. A comment sits under its line in a glass-control card that keeps to the visible width however far long code lines scroll; its editor takes Enter to add and Esc to cancel, and focus returns to the line. Once no agent works in the folder, each change's band carries a ghost Revert at the right of the visible width (the fold's band before it, or a band of its own), and its confirmation names the file, the line and the +/− lines that change back. ## Motion and accessibility

The website's motion is rich but purposeful, and built from named primitives:

- **Primitives.** Rise, word reveal, letter roll, typing and streaming, handoff pass, glide, flatten, glint, magnetic pull and marquee.
- **Easing.** Expo `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for entrances and glide `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` for moving indicators.
- **Pausing.** Ambient loops pause offscreen and in hidden tabs (the enhancement layer marks offscreen zones with `data-off` and the page with `data-hidden`; a loop declared with a more specific selector than its hold uses animation longhands, so no `animation` shorthand resets the held play state). The capability cycle also pauses while the section is in use.
- **Fallbacks.** Pointer effects run only with a fine pointer. Reduced motion shows static, complete compositions, and without JavaScript the page shows the final composition. There is no canvas.

Desktop motion stays event-driven, with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`:

- Hover 120ms, press 80ms, popover 160ms in and 120ms out with at most 2px travel, panel 180ms.
- Glides use 240ms. Pressed buttons scale to 0.97.
- Dictation: each microphone report slides the waveform one bar in `--g-dur-wave` (80ms), a new word clears from a 4px blur in 240ms, Finish's sweep takes `--g-dur-ink` (560ms) and Cancel's dissolve `--g-dur-dissolve` (320ms).

Reduced motion and the in-app motion setting remove movement and duration. State changes must stay understandable without animation. Preserve visible keyboard focus, native dialog behavior, accessible names, error announcements, readable contrast, and robust zoom and reflow.

## Governance and verification

For future visual work, read this document and [reference-audit.md](reference-audit.md), then inspect the shared tokens and production components. Until the owner locks Halo, visual artifacts are proposals. Record the owner's approval and artifact locations before treating them as golden. For a new feature, extend the semantic tokens and interaction patterns, show desktop states in light and dark and website states in light, and preserve accessibility and behavior. Update this document when an owner decision changes the design; do not leave competing directions in plans or READMEs.

Run `pnpm check` and `pnpm build`, `pnpm --filter @auracode/web test:browser`, and the desktop `check:chat`, `check:setup`, `check:settings` and `check:customization` suites as appropriate to changed surfaces. `check:settings` includes `check-themes.mjs`: the gallery, keyboard, persistence, a failed save and axe for every theme in both appearances. Run `node apps/desktop/scripts/check-layout.mjs` in both engines, both appearances and both window sizes. Add `--glass worst` to repeat it over the worst glass backdrop.

Development previews take `&glass=acrylic` to emulate the window material over a colourful desktop. `&backdrop=worst` paints the worst plausible material instead. Verify both desktop themes, system switching, saved desktop preferences, portal and terminal updates, the website's light appearance under a dark system preference, keyboard use, reduced motion, responsive sizes and contrast. Browser fixtures and screenshots do not establish native macOS or Windows release acceptance; verify native materials, menus and title controls on supported platforms separately. Existing style checks enforce token usage; use objective checks for meaningful invariants rather than freezing arbitrary layouts.
